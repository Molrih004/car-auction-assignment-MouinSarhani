# Case: VeloVault — Classic Car Auction Platform

## Background

VeloVault is a mid-sized classic car dealership based in Middelburg. For the past twelve years the company has brokered the sale of rare and vintage automobiles — mostly European makes from the 1950s through the 1980s — to a loyal but growing community of collectors and enthusiasts across the Netherlands and Belgium.

Until recently, VeloVault's auction process was entirely manual. A seller would contact the company by phone or email, a staff member would photograph and list the car in a shared spreadsheet, and interested buyers would submit bids by replying to a newsletter. When the auction window closed, a staff member would tally the bids, notify the winner, and arrange transfer of ownership. The process worked well when the company handled a dozen auctions per month, but the business has grown significantly. VeloVault now receives requests for forty to sixty auctions per month, and the manual process is no longer sustainable.

## The Digital Platform

The management team decided to commission a digital platform that would allow sellers to list their own cars, set auction terms, and let buyers browse and bid without staff involvement. A development team was contracted to build a first working version.

That first version is now running. It consists of three backend services:

- An **authentication service** — handles user registration and login
- A **cars catalog service** — allows cars to be listed and managed
- An **API gateway** — the single entry point clients use to reach all three services

## Your Assignment

VeloVault is preparing a **second development sprint**. Before the development team begins, the product owner wants a clear and agreed-upon set of requirements — something the first sprint lacked. You have been brought in as a **junior business analyst** to interview the key stakeholders and produce a requirements document.

Your starting point is a set of stakeholder interviews conducted during an initial scoping session. Your task is to analyse these interviews and create the following products:

1. Persona
2. Journey map
3. System requirements
4. 2 System Context Diagrams (UCD and Level 0 DFD)
5. Wireflow
6. High fidelity prototype (please pay attention to heuristics)
7. Use-case specification (2 filled in Use Case templates per student)
8. 1 Activity diagram of 1 of the Use Cases per student
9. 1 Entity Relationship Diagram
10. 1 UML Component diagram of the Microservice Architecture
11. API message design (logical data object) and API Specification per microservice.
12. UXD Testing (test script, questions and findings)
